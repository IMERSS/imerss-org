### [Four winners share the 2026 Ebbe Nielsen Challenge award](https://www.gbif.org/news/4eaECI5j379IJuVnztZfAF/four-winners-share-the-2026-ebbe-nielsen-challenge-award)
![bioReportR team](images/bioReportR_team.jpg#floatleft)

The Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF) named bioReportR, developed by Emma Menchions, Antranig Basman and Andrew Simon of IMERSS, one of four winners of the 2026 Ebbe Nielsen Challenge, selected from entries across 31 countries. bioReportR is a customizable, community-driven tool for regional biodiversity reporting and monitoring, bringing together occurrence data from GBIF and other sources to explore biodiversity patterns, observation effort, protected areas and community science contributions. It also includes an extirpation-probability component, building on our [protocol for the detection of species extirpation](https://imerss.github.io/detecting-local-extinction/docs/).

Explore [bioReportR on GEO BON's BON in a Box](https://boninabox.geobon.org/tool-detail?id=251), or find the code on [GitHub](https://github.com/IMERSS/bioReportR). Image: the bioReportR team, Emma Menchions, Antranig Basman and Andrew Simon (GBIF).

### [Revealing the invisible: a new baseline for Salish Sea diatoms answers a global call](https://www.the-microbiologist.com/news/revealing-the-invisible-a-new-baseline-for-salish-sea-diatoms-answers-a-global-call/8960.article)
![Trigonium quinquelobatum](images/Trigonium_live.jpg#floatleft)

An article by Linda Stewart in *The Microbiologist* featuring our [annotated checklist of Salish Sea diatoms](https://doi.org/10.3897/BDJ.14.e189060), published in *Biodiversity Data Journal* by Mark Webber and colleagues. The article describes how our checklist of 924 diatom taxa and curated dataset of 11,469 records fills a long-standing gap in the region's fragmented historical records, establishing a baseline to support environmental monitoring in a sea facing urban growth, industrial activity and heavy shipping traffic.

Image: *Trigonium quinquelobatum*, live cell (LM; Webber et al. 2026, CC BY 4.0).

### [Shining the light on baby crabs](https://hakaimagazine.com/features/shining-the-light-on-baby-crabs/)
![light trap](images/J9_LightTraps.jpeg#floatleft)

A Hakai Magazine article by Spoorthy Raman featuring the Hakai Institute’s [Sentinels of Change](https://sentinels.hakai.org/) program, which is co-coordinated on Galiano Island by Jeannine Georgeson (IMERSS) and the Galiano Conservancy.

“For Georgeson, who is at the Whaler Bay dock almost every other day in the spring and summer to work with the trap, the motivation to be involved in the light trap monitoring project goes beyond science. It’s a way to connect with her culture, in which crabs hold a dear place, and to preserve family traditions, she says. She likens the work to her efforts to learn Hul’q’umín’um’, her family’s Coast Salish language. I ask her if she’ll continue to be involved in the project next year. ‘I’d love to do this for another 10 years,’ she says if that means preserving the Dungeness crab and its cultural significance for future generations. ‘I want my granddaughter to know what it’s like to be able to be a part of this.’”
